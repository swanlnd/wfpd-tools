Choosing a Budgeting Tool

There are dozens of budgeting apps. The most important thing isn't which is prettiest — it's how much of your private money data it touches, and who can see it.

🔒 Your money data is yours — keep it that way.

Whatever you choose for this program, your salary and spending stay in your own hands. Nothing you track is collected by SWAN or anyone at work. That's a promise — and it's also why we lead with a sheet you copy into your own Google account.

01

The one design choice that protects you

📄

Make your own copy

Our budget template is something you copy into your own Google Drive (File → Make a copy). From that moment it's your private sheet — your numbers never flow to a shared company form, and no one else can open it. If a tool ever asks everyone to enter data into one shared place, that's the opposite of what you want.

02

The privacy ladder

A simple way to think about any tool: the more it automates, the more of your data it touches. "Free" often means your data is the product. Here's the trade-off, safest first — names are examples, not endorsements.

Most private

A spreadsheet you own

Your data lives only in your Google Drive. Nothing is linked to your bank, nothing is sold. You do a little manual entry — that's the small price of full privacy.

Middle ground

Manual-entry apps

Nicer interfaces than a sheet, and many work without linking your bank if you enter spending yourself. Lighter data footprint — but you're still trusting the app maker, so read the privacy policy.

Biggest trade-off

Bank-linking apps

The most convenient — they auto-import transactions — but they connect to your bank accounts (usually through a third-party "aggregator"). That's exactly the data-sharing to be deliberate about. Only if you're comfortable with it.

03

Questions to ask before trusting any app

  • Bank login? Does it need it — or can I enter manually?
  • Where's my data? Stored where, and who can see it?
  • Sold or shared? Does the privacy policy allow it?
  • Can I leave? Export my data and delete my account?
  • Who's behind it? Reputable company, clear track record?
  • Free — why? Ads, upsell, or is the data the product?

The rule of thumb: when in doubt, a spreadsheet you own is the safest home for your money data. Convenience and privacy trade off against each other — pick the point on the ladder you're comfortable with.

App names are examples for illustration, not endorsements, and their features and privacy policies change over time — always check the current policy yourself. This is general education from SWAN L&D & Finance, not financial advice, and no tool here is required or monitored by your employer.
